# Engineering Ladder

## Overview

The performance characteristics described can be useful in conversations with your manager around quarterly goal setting.
The document is not meant to be used as a checklist for promotion. Evaluate levels and their performance characteristics holistically.


Each level includes the expectations of the levels before it. The path runs from tactical (guide a team to deliver its part of the whole) to operational (coordinate many efforts within a strategic goal) to strategic (define why, and with what, the organization will succeed).

- **Scope of influence is not just headcount.** A small, highly specialized team can carry large scope. Scope usually shows in the depth and breadth of the org tree, in how visible the leader is across the organization, and in how few hops separate the work from company goals.
- **Sustainable results.** Results never come at the expense of long-term growth, retention, or psychological safety.
- **Personal growth at every level.** Seek feedback, know your strengths and gaps, and invest in becoming a better manager and leader.


## Levels

| Level | Title | Summary | Typical scope | Dropbox equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M3 | Engineering Manager | Accountable for the performance and results of a small team of ICs. Owns the operational, people, and roadmap aspects of the area and stays close to customer needs. | One or two teams of ICs. More than half of time on management. Sets objectives, runs reviews, proposes compensation. | M3–M4 |
| M4 | Senior Engineering Manager | Accountable for the budget, performance, and results of a medium team or several small teams. Sets the bar for operational, people, and roadmap work and coaches other managers. | A complex area or two medium ones, possibly multi-site. Full authority for reviews, pay, hiring, and exits. May own cost centers. | M5 |
| M5 | Director of Engineering | Accountable for the performance, results, and strategic direction of a department. Leads managers and expert ICs. | Manager of managers. Sets department priorities. Sizable budget; makes investment calls relative to other areas. Needs little direction. | M6 |
| M6 | Senior Director of Engineering | Accountable for a large organization, such as a major product line or a global function. Drives outcomes through other leaders. | Significant business area, often global. Reports to a VP or SVP. Major influence on operating expenses. | M7 |
| M7 | Vice President of Engineering | Accountable for all of engineering. Drives outcomes through other leaders. | Significant business area, often global. Owns operating expenses. | M8 |
| M8 | CTO | Accountable for success of the business via technology and the engineering org. | C-Suite member, often a co-founder or previous experience making existential decisions. (Whether the CTO also owns the people organization varies by organization.) | |

Analogy:

- M3 captains a small crew on day trips
- M4 takes a mid-sized ship on longer voyages through rough water
- M5 takes a large ship on long voyages through dangerous waters
- M6 directs a fleet through a storm
- M7 decides the number and sizes of fleets
- M8 designs ports, fleets, ships


## Role essentials

### Where the time goes

1. **Directing:** ensure engineering efforts are aligned with leadership and business needs.
   - Meet with peers, your "first team" (TODO: link "So you're a people manager, who is your first team?").
   - Communicate context and vision down to ICs, and challenges and opportunities up to leadership.
2. **Measuring and feedback:** keep engineering accountable to business deliverables and to engineering KPIs.

### The three most important duties

1. **Alignment:** understand what the organization and leadership need, and communicate it to your teams. This takes both results and vision and strategy.
2. **Effective frameworks, standards, and processes:** improve or remove what is inefficient; add process when necessary. Manage access and permissions to critical systems, such as the code repository, including adherence to HIPAA, SOC 2, and similar requirements.
3. **Balancing stakeholder needs:** customers, product and engineering teams, compliance, finance, and others. Be the inbound path for new requests and projects into engineering.

### Skills

- Understand technology and engineering: what is feasible, what is productive, and where there is more opportunity.
- Communicate effectively.
- Build relationships and trust.
- Deliver results and impact.

### Measures of success

- The company hits its OKRs, and company KPIs trend in the right direction.
- Engineering hits its OKRs, and engineering KPIs trend in the right direction.
- Teams deliver on their roadmaps and hit their OKRs.

## Core competencies

### Results

| Competency | M3 Engineering Manager | M4 Senior Engineering Manager | M5 Director | M6 Senior Director |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope of Influence | Team | Area (multiple teams) | Department | Organization |
| Characteristics | Deliver well-defined projects toward quarterly goals, with guidance. | Independently deliver multiple workstreams over six to twelve months. | Own results for a strategic objective tied to company goals over one to two years. | Own results for a portfolio of strategic objectives over two to three years. |
| Delivery | Lead the team to ship high-quality work that matters most to customers and the business; simplify and cut unnecessary work. | Deliver across several projects at a high standard; find new opportunities with wider impact. | Deliver through managers; the department's work contributes directly to company success or failure. | Deliver through directors; step in only where personal attention is required. |
| Goals and Metrics | Set team goals and metrics at the right altitude; the team hits its OKRs. | Set goals and key metrics across teams; improve how customer impact is measured and communicated. | Define department OKRs and engineering KPIs that roll up to company goals with few hops. | Company and engineering KPIs trend in the right direction; set the metrics the organization is judged by. |
| Ownership | Own commitments and the team's technical decisions; learn from mistakes; escalate appropriately. | Drive urgency on incidents; eliminate root causes and toil. | Inspect, oversee, and prune the project portfolio; hold managers accountable. | Hold directors accountable; shut down initiatives that do not create value. |
| Decision Making | Gather input and make timely decisions; understand risk and impact. | Reach the right decision despite conflicting perspectives; balance detail with the big picture. | Act decisively in critical situations, including unpopular calls; prioritize investment across the portfolio. | Make decisions with company-wide implications; restructure roles and goals when results stall. |
| Process | Run efficient team cadences; improve team process. | Add, change, or remove process as complexity grows; coach other managers on it. | Create and adapt processes as the organization evolves, including agile practices, engineering practices, and tooling; align them across teams and other departments. | Shape how engineering operates as a whole; balance operational excellence, innovation, and speed. |

### Technology

| Competency | M3 Engineering Manager | M4 Senior Engineering Manager | M5 Director | M6 Senior Director |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope of Influence | Team systems | Area systems | Department systems | Organization technical direction |
| Characteristics | Stay close to the code; accountable for the team's technical decisions and architecture tradeoffs. | Review design and health across an area; hands-on work is optional. | Step away from committing code; ensure technical health through leads, reviews, and metrics. | Shape technical direction with principal engineers and the CTO. |
| Technical Judgment | Give quality design feedback; surface wrong assumptions and technical risk early. | Know what is feasible and productive across teams; resolve cross-team dependencies early. | See where the larger opportunities are; make build, buy, or reuse tradeoffs with staff engineers. | See around the bend: anticipate future technical problems for the organization. |
| Domain Expertise | Know the team's domain, such as applications and business logic, or data. | Be the go-to leader for the area's domain. | Understand how domains across the department interact. | Connect domains across the organization to business strategy. |
| Quality and Stability | Balance features with tech debt, testing, and simplification. | Require health reviews, test strategy, scaling reviews, and safe rollouts and deprecations. | Keep systems stable ("stay the course"); review health metrics across the department. | Set quality and reliability standards for the organization. |
| Technical Strategy | Align the team's systems with technical strategy. | Hold the area to technical strategy, making tradeoffs with staff engineers. | Contribute to technical strategy with staff and principal engineers. | Co-own technical strategy with principal engineers and the CTO. |
| Innovation and Industry Awareness | Encourage experiments; avoid reinventing the wheel. | Prioritize innovations that measurably lower maintenance or cost; follow relevant vendor announcements, such as AWS re:Invent and GitHub. | Decide where to innovate and where to optimize for efficiency. | Form informed opinions on industry trends the company should adopt or guard against. |
| Security and Compliance | Follow access controls and compliance practices such as HIPAA and SOC 2. | Manage access and permissions to critical systems, such as the code repository and production; keep the area audit-ready. | Own the department's compliance posture with security and compliance partners. | Set engineering policy with security, legal, and compliance leaders. |
| AI / LLM | Ensure the team uses the Org's AI tools well and understands limits such as hallucination and data privacy. | Integrate AI into team workflows, such as code review, documentation, and agents; measure the effect. | Drive AI adoption across teams; set guardrails for data and privacy; measure productivity impact. | Set engineering's AI strategy, aligned with company strategy and vendor direction. |

### Collaboration

| Competency | M3 Engineering Manager | M4 Senior Engineering Manager | M5 Director | M6 Senior Director |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope of Influence | Team and cross-functional partners (product, design, QA) | Multiple teams and cross-functional partners | Peer managers and other departments | Company leaders and external forums |
| Characteristics | Build healthy partnerships with long-lived partners. | Influence outcomes across teams, often through conflict and ambiguity. | Influence without authority across departments. | Navigate organizational dynamics among company leaders; represent engineering externally. |
| Peers (First Team) | Build relationships with peer managers. | Treat peer managers as the first team; optimize for the organization over the team. | Treat peer directors and cross-functional heads as the first team. | Treat the leadership team as the first team; align with VPs and executives. |
| Stakeholders and Intake | Understand stakeholder needs for the team's work. | Balance product, design, and customer needs across teams; manage incoming requests. | Be the inbound path for new requests and projects into engineering; balance customers, product, compliance, and finance. | Balance stakeholder needs at the company level. |
| Communication | Communicate clearly for the audience; champion the team and its members. | Turn company updates and decisions into context for teams; share information proactively. | Communicate context and vision to ICs, and challenges and opportunities to leadership; write crisp narratives. | Communicate complex or unpopular ideas with conviction and gain commitment; be visible across the organization. |
| Conflict and Alignment | Engage in productive conflict; disagree and commit. | Break down silos; resolve misalignment with partners. | Resolve cross-engineering misalignment on strategic choices. | Remove barriers to collaboration across the company. |

### People

| Competency | M3 Engineering Manager | M4 Senior Engineering Manager | M5 Director | M6 Senior Director |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope of Influence | ICs | ICs, senior ICs, and sometimes other managers | Managers and expert ICs | Managers of managers |
| Characteristics | Full management responsibility for a small team; more than half of time on management. | Lead a medium-to-large team; coach other managers. | Hire and develop managers; lead through others. | Develop leaders; own the full talent lifecycle for a large organization. |
| Hiring | Hire across a broad range of backgrounds and experience; interview actively. | Plan team headcount; calibrate interviewers; represent the mission to candidates. | Hire managers and senior ICs; improve hiring processes and standards. | Attract senior leaders and build followership; hire directors. |
| Coaching and Career Growth | Give timely, direct, actionable feedback; offer next-level assignments. | Grow senior ICs and find them leveraged roles beyond the team. | Grow managers who run their teams well. | Grow managers who grow managers; build a pipeline of future leaders. |
| Performance Management | Set clear expectations; address performance issues with support. | Manage high and low performance independently, including pay, discipline, and exits. | Calibrate performance across teams; hold managers to consistent standards. | Set performance standards and champion a high-performance culture across the organization. |
| Org Design | Structure roles on the team; staff projects with the manager's input. | Staff projects independently; put the strongest technical leaders where they matter most. | Structure the department, including team splits, new managers, and reporting lines; plan succession. | Structure a complex organization against a portfolio of investments; build the bench for key roles. |
| Culture and Engagement | Create psychological safety and an inclusive team; support well-being. | Keep a pulse on engagement; retain proactively. | Inspire and motivate; debug most organizational health issues. | Build an engaging, successful organization people want to join; resolve the hardest organizational health issues. |

### Vision and Strategy

| Competency | M3 Engineering Manager | M4 Senior Engineering Manager | M5 Director | M6 Senior Director |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope of Influence | Team | Area | Department | Organization |
| Characteristics | Tactical: execute a strategy mostly defined by others. | Define the area's strategy within the organization's direction. | Operational: own the how for a department within strategic goals. | Strategic: help define the why and with what. |
| Alignment | Understand what leadership and the business need, explain it to the team, and build buy-in. | Align team strategy with stakeholders, company priorities, and customer needs. | Ensure engineering efforts across the department align with leadership and business needs. | Align the organization to company strategy and help shape that strategy. |
| Planning Horizon | Quarterly priorities; balance keeping the lights on, foundation work, and new initiatives. | Six-to-twelve-month roadmap across workstreams. | One-to-two-year plan for a strategic objective. | Two-to-three-year vision for a portfolio of objectives. |
| Business and Customer | Understand customers and how the team's work creates value. | Use customer insight to shape strategy. | Understand the business and market; define long-term vision with cross-functional partners. | Inform company business and product strategy. |
| Org Strategy | — | Propose structural changes as scope grows. | Choose the department's shape, including team topology, locations, and contractors. | Choose organizational models, such as international expansion, contractors and vendors, flat or hierarchical, general-manager (vertical), or matrix. |
| Investment and Budget | Understand the team's costs, such as headcount, tools, and cloud. | Own the budget for the team or teams; may manage cost centers. | Decide investment levels relative to other areas; move resources outside the area when the business needs it. | Hold major financial oversight or influence on operating expenses. |
| Change Management | Keep the team steady through change. | Introduce change and guide teams through it. | Drive significant organizational changes that improve throughput, retention, and customer outcomes. | Drive transformation, such as shifting engineering from on-premise products to cloud SaaS. |
